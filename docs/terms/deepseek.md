# DeepSeek: storing, sharing and publishing outputs

**Not legal advice.** A summary of DeepSeek's published terms as fetched on
2026-11-26, for the decisions in [README.md](README.md). Models in scope:
`deepseek-v4-pro`, `deepseek-flash` ([models.yaml](../../models.yaml)). Quotes
are short and verbatim (typographic quotes changed to ASCII); everything under
"Reading" is interpretation.

## Documents read

| Document | Covers | Date shown |
|---|---|---|
| [DeepSeek Open Platform Terms of Service](https://cdn.deepseek.com/policies/en-US/deepseek-open-platform-terms-of-service.html) | API | released Apr 22, 2026; effective Apr 29, 2026 |
| [DeepSeek Terms of Use](https://cdn.deepseek.com/policies/en-US/deepseek-terms-of-use.html) | all services, API included | last update Mar 27, 2026 |
| [DeepSeek Privacy Policy](https://cdn.deepseek.com/policies/en-US/deepseek-privacy-policy.html) | personal data | last update Feb 10, 2026 |

The Open Platform Terms are "a Specific Agreement to the "DeepSeek Terms of
Use"", and developers must comply with the Terms of Use; where the two
conflict, the Specific Terms prevail. A UK-English edition of the Open Platform
Terms (same dates, brand written "DSeek") was compared word by word: it differs
in naming, section numbering and small wording changes in §5.5 and §7.1, none
about outputs. The copy at
platform.deepseek.com/downloads returned HTTP 403 and was not read.

## What the text says: API

- **Ownership.** Open Platform Terms §4.2: "We assign any rights, title, and
  interests—if any—in the Outputs of the Services to you".
- **Uses allowed.** Same clause: you may use Inputs and Outputs for "personal
  use, academic research, derivative product development, training other
  models (such as model distillation), etc." No competing-model or benchmarking
  restriction was found in either terms document.
- **Publishing.** Terms of Use §3.1: "If you publish or disseminate outputs
  generated by the Services, you must: (1) proactively verify the authenticity
  and accuracy of the output content ...; (2) clearly indicate that the output
  content is generated by artificial intelligence ...; (3) avoid publishing and
  disseminating any output content that violates the usage specifications".
- **Disclosure to end users.** Open Platform Terms §8.1: "You shall clearly
  disclose to your end users that the Output content is generated by AI, and
  may contain errors or omissions and are for reference only." Terms of Use
  §5.3: you "shall not delete, alter, forge, conceal, or attempt to circumvent"
  DeepSeek's AI-generated labels.
- **Naming DeepSeek.** Open Platform Terms §5.2: "Without our permission, you or
  your end users shall not use any trademarks, ... trade names, ... including
  but not limited to "DeepSeek," etc., in any way". §5.3 lists the improper uses
  in view, such as implying partnership or endorsement.
- **Training on API data.** The Open Platform Terms say nothing about it. The
  Terms of Use §4.3: DeepSeek "may, to a minimal extent, use Inputs and Outputs
  to provide, maintain, operate, develop or improve the Services", after
  de-identification; the opt-out is "turning off "Improve the model for
  everyone"". The Privacy Policy lists use "to train and improve our technology,
  such as our machine learning models".
- **Retention.** No period for API data is given. Privacy Policy: input data is
  kept "for as long as you have an account", and personal data is stored "in
  People's Republic of China."
- **Region and law.** Operator: Hangzhou DeepSeek Artificial Intelligence Co.,
  Ltd. Governing law: "the laws of the People's Republic of China in the
  mainland", with suits in the court where DeepSeek is registered (§10). No
  warranty "that the Services are available or will continue to be available in
  certain jurisdictions" (§1.4). Terms change "seven (7) days after release"
  (§11.1).

## What the text says: chat product

DeepSeek's chat product is not part of T5.1 (plan.md names Claude, ChatGPT and
Gemini). For reference, the Terms of Use forbid "capturing, copying any content
of the Services, including but not limited to using any robots, spiders, or
other automatic setups" (§3.5(3)).

## Reading (not legal advice)

- DeepSeek is the most permissive on use: ownership is assigned and training
  other models is expressly allowed, so publishing a dataset raises no
  third-party-training concern.
- It is also the only provider whose terms impose a duty on publication: every
  published DeepSeek output must be marked as AI-generated and checked for
  accuracy first. For a dataset of default outputs, "verify the authenticity and
  accuracy" cannot mean correcting them (sibar stores them unedited); a clear
  notice that outputs are unverified AI text is the nearest fit, but the text
  does not say whether that suffices.
- §5.2 read literally forbids using the name "DeepSeek" "in any way" without
  permission, which would cover naming the model in a public record. §5.3
  suggests the aim is brand misuse, but there is no exception for factual
  reference.
- Whether API prompts and outputs are used for training is not settled by the
  text: the API terms are silent and the general opt-out is described as an
  account setting.
- Data is stored in China under PRC law; whether that is acceptable for sibar is
  a decision for the Human, though sibar sends only public prompts.
